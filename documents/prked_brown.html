Decoding the Dorms: An Insider's Guide to the Best Places to Live at Brown University

Hey there, future Bruin! So, you got into Brown. First off, HUGE congrats. You're about to embark on an incredible journey at one of the most unique & exciting universities out there. But before you start mapping out your classes on the Main Green or planning your first trip to Thayer Street, there's one BIG question you need to answer: where are you going to live?
Honestly, choosing a dorm at Brown is a rite of passage. It's more than just a place to crash after a late-night study session at the SciLi; it's your first community, your home base, & the backdrop for some of your most memorable college moments. And let me tell you, the dorms at Brown are as diverse & full of character as the students themselves.
I’ve been around the block, lived in a few of these places, & heard all the stories from friends. So, I’m here to give you the real-deal, no-fluff guide to Brown’s dorms. We're going to dive deep into the nitty-gritty of freshman housing, explore the world of upperclassman living, & even touch on the unique opportunities in program houses & Greek life. By the end of this, you'll have a much better idea of what to expect & which dorms might be the perfect fit for you.

The Freshman Experience: A Tale of Two Campuses

As a first-year at Brown, you don't get to pick your exact dorm, but you'll be placed in one of the designated freshman areas. The vast majority of first-years are sorted into two main areas: Pembroke Campus & Keeney Quad. Each has its own distinct vibe, & your experience will be shaped in part by which one you call home.

Pembroke Campus: The "Chill" Side of First-Year Life

Located on the north side of campus, Pembroke is known for being a bit more laid-back than its southern counterpart. It’s home to a cluster of freshman dorms, the Verney-Woolley (V-Dub) dining hall, & is conveniently close to the Nelson Fitness Center & Thayer Street. If you're looking for a slightly calmer, more tight-knit community feel, Pembroke might be your jam.

Andrews Hall: The "Hotel" of Freshman Dorms

If you hear someone refer to "Hotel Andrews," don't be confused. They're talking about Andrews Hall, & for good reason. This dorm is one of the most sought-after freshman residences, largely due to its recently renovated rooms & amazing amenities. The rooms in Andrews are known for being spacious, with high ceilings & even walk-in closets in some cases. But the real game-changer? Many rooms have their own sinks, a luxury you won't find in most other freshman dorms.
Living in Andrews also means you're just a few steps away from Andrews Commons, a dining hall right in the building. This is a HUGE plus, especially on those cold or rainy Providence days when you don't want to venture far for a meal. The dorm also has great study spaces, making it easy to buckle down & get work done without having to trek to the library.
  • Vibe: Modern, comfortable, & convenient.
  • Pros: Spacious rooms, in-room sinks, walk-in closets, attached dining hall, great study spots.
  • Cons: Can be a bit quieter than Keeney, but that might be a pro for some!

Metcalf & Miller Halls: Andrews' "Little Siblings"

Right next to Andrews, you'll find Metcalf & Miller Halls. These dorms were also recently renovated, so they share a similar new & clean feel. While the rooms might not have the in-room sinks or walk-in closets of Andrews, they are still considered very nice & are a great place to spend your first year. You get all the benefits of being on Pembroke Campus, including the proximity to the V-Dub & the gym.
  • Vibe: Similar to Andrews, but a bit more standard in terms of room amenities.
  • Pros: Recently renovated, good-sized rooms, close to Andrews Commons.
  • Cons: No in-room sinks, which can be a bummer after you've heard about the ones in Andrews!

Emery-Woolley (Em-Wool) & Morriss-Champlin (Mo-Champ): The Hidden Gems

For a long time, Em-Wool & Mo-Champ were considered the less desirable options on Pembroke. But here's the thing: they've both gotten some serious upgrades in recent years, & many students who live there end up LOVING it.
Em-Wool’s biggest selling point is the semi-private bathrooms. Instead of a large communal bathroom for the whole floor, you’ll share a bathroom with just a few other rooms. This is a MAJOR perk for a freshman dorm. The rooms themselves are a decent size, & the location right above the V-Dub is super convenient.
Mo-Champ also has a lot going for it. After its renovation, it boasts spacious rooms & a fantastic lounge with a fireplace, pool table, & ping pong table. This lounge has become a popular study & social spot, giving Mo-Champ a real sense of community.
  • Vibe: Up-and-coming, community-focused, & surprisingly awesome.
  • Pros: Semi-private bathrooms (Em-Wool), great lounge (Mo-Champ), close to the V-Dub.
  • Cons: Some rooms can feel a bit "boxy" or basement-like, but the recent renovations have made a big difference.

Keeney Quad: The Social Hub of Freshman Year

If Pembroke is the "chill" side of freshman life, Keeney Quad is the "social" side. Located on the south side of campus near the Main Green & the Rockefeller Library (the "Rock"), Keeney is a massive complex of four connected dorms: Archibald-Bronson, Everett-Poland, Jameson-Mead, & Wriston. It's known for being loud, social, & the place where there's always something going on.
The rooms in Keeney can be a bit of a mixed bag. Some are surprisingly spacious, especially the corner rooms, while others are on the smaller side. But what you might lack in personal space, you make up for in social opportunities. The hallways are always buzzing with activity, & it's incredibly easy to meet people.
One thing to be aware of is that Keeney has a reputation for being a bit… well-loved. It’s a party dorm, & the common areas & bathrooms can sometimes show it. But for many, that's all part of the classic freshman experience. You’ll have a blast, make a ton of friends, & have some wild stories to tell.
  • Vibe: Social, energetic, & always active.
  • Pros: The best place to be if you want a super social freshman year, centrally located, easy to meet people.
  • Cons: Can be loud, rooms can be smaller, communal bathrooms can get messy.

Upperclassman Living: The Quest for the Perfect Pad

Once you've survived your freshman year, a whole new world of housing options opens up. Starting sophomore year, you'll enter the infamous housing lottery. This is where you & a group of friends get a randomly assigned time slot to pick your dorm for the following year. It can be a stressful process, but it's also your chance to find a living situation that's a perfect fit for your friend group & your lifestyle.

The Housing Lottery: A Game of Chance & Strategy

The housing lottery is a classic Brown experience. Here's a quick rundown of how it works:
  1. Form a Group: You'll team up with the people you want to live with. The size of your group will depend on whether you're aiming for a suite, a triple, a double, or a collection of singles.
  2. Get Your Number: Your group will be assigned a random lottery number, which determines your pick time. Seniors get the best numbers, followed by juniors, & then sophomores.
  3. Make a Plan (and a Backup Plan... and a Backup-Backup Plan): Before your pick time, you & your group will scour the available rooms & make a list of your top choices. It's crucial to have multiple backup plans, as the most desirable rooms get snapped up quickly.
The lottery can be a bit of a nail-biter, but it's also a great way to learn about all the different upperclassman dorms on campus.

The Best of the Best: Coveted Upperclassman Dorms

While there are tons of great upperclassman dorms, a few stand out as being particularly popular.

Young Orchard & Hegeman: Suite Life at its Finest

If you're looking for suite-style living, Young Orchard & Hegeman are at the top of many students' lists. These dorms offer suites with multiple single rooms, a common room, & sometimes even a private bathroom. They're a great option for a group of friends who want to live together but still have their own personal space. Because they're so popular, they can be tough to get into, especially as a sophomore.

Gregorian Quads: A Modern & Social Option

The Gregorian Quads (A & B) are another popular choice, offering a mix of singles, doubles, & suites. The rooms are modern & well-maintained, & the quads have a social atmosphere without being as rowdy as Keeney. The location is also great, with easy access to both the main campus & Thayer Street.

Slater Hall: History & Views on the Main Green

For those who want a taste of Brown's history, Slater Hall is a fantastic option. Located right on the Main Green, it's one of the most picturesque dorms on campus. The rooms can be a bit quirky due to the age of the building, but many of them have amazing views & a ton of character. It's a great choice for upperclassmen who want a more traditional dorm experience in a prime location.

Off-Campus Living: The Ultimate Freedom

By the time you're a junior or senior, you also have the option to move off-campus. This is a popular choice for many students, as it offers a greater sense of independence & a chance to explore Providence beyond College Hill.
Living off-campus comes with its own set of challenges, though. You'll have to deal with landlords, bills, & finding furniture. And then there's the issue of parking. Beyond the Traditional Dorm: Program Houses & Greek Life

If you're looking for a more unique living experience, Brown offers a variety of program houses & Greek life options. These are communities organized around a shared interest, identity, or cultural background.

Program Houses: Living with Your Passions

Program houses are a really cool part of Brown's residential life. They're student-led communities where you can live with people who share your interests. There are houses for everything from technology (Tech House) & French language (French House) to social justice & the arts. Living in a program house is a great way to immerse yourself in a specific community & make friends who are passionate about the same things you are.

Greek Life: A Different Kind of Community

Brown's Greek life is a bit different from what you might see at other schools. The fraternities & sororities are all housed on Wriston Quad, creating a centralized hub for Greek activity. While Greek life is a part of the social scene at Brown, it's not as dominant as it is at some other universities. If you're interested in joining a fraternity or sorority, it's a great way to find a tight-knit community & get involved in philanthropy & social events.

So, What's the "Best" Dorm at Brown?

Here's the honest truth: there's no single "best" dorm at Brown. The right dorm for you really depends on what you're looking for.
  • If you want a classic, social freshman experience: Keeney Quad is probably your best bet.
  • If you're looking for something a bit more modern & comfortable: Andrews Hall is the way to go.
  • If you value privacy & a quieter atmosphere: Em-Wool or Mo-Champ could be a perfect fit.
  • As an upperclassman, if you want to live with a group of friends in a suite: Aim for Young Orchard or Hegeman in the housing lottery.
  • If you're looking for a unique, interest-based community: Definitely check out the program houses.
Ultimately, your dorm is just one piece of your Brown experience. No matter where you end up, you'll have a chance to make friends, build a community, & create a home for yourself on College Hill.
I hope this was helpful in giving you a better sense of the residential landscape at Brown. It's a lot to take in, but it's also an exciting part of the journey. Let me know what you think, & good luck with your housing adventure!
