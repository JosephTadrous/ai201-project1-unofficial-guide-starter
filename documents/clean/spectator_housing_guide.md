# The ultimate guide to first-year housing

First-year housing can be tricky. Before you get to Columbia and have plenty of people to guide you, how do you know which dorm is the best for you? Sometimes, never-ending Reddit wormholes and outdated articles just don’t cut it. Here’s Spectrum’s comprehensive guide to first-year housing that will tell you more than just “Carman is social” and “Wallach is quiet.”

## Wallach Hall

Located on lower campus on Amsterdam Avenue and overlooking the main lawn, Wallach is a classic first-year dorm with plenty of amenities for first-year students. Wallach has 141 singles and 51 doubles, leading to a mostly quiet atmosphere.

**Pros:**

- Both John Jay Dining Hall and JJ’s Place are connected to the building, and while this may seem small, it really comes in handy during the winter months or when you are sick. It can be nice to grab a quick meal without having to put on a coat or scarf.
- The rooms (particularly the corner rooms) are on the bigger side.
- Bathrooms are single-use and cleaned regularly; each floor has bathrooms in the hallway with a sink, shower, and toilet in each unit.
- It’s quiet, which is good for someone who plans to spend a lot of time working in their dorm.
- The laundry room is typically pretty empty, and you can almost always find a machine that’s not in use.
- There’s an esports game lounge downstairs, which is pretty cool if you’re into that sort of thing.
- The lounges on most floors are spacious and have plenty of natural light, and each has a kitchen, which is great if you plan to cook.

**Cons:**

- It’s not the most social. There likely won’t be many parties or crowded hallways on your floor. That being said, this does not impede your ability to have a social life! It just won’t be happening in your dorm building.
- The basement laundry room requires you to walk through a long hallway, and there’s nowhere to sit or hang out while you wash your clothes.
- You don’t have your own bathroom.

## John Jay Hall

With 395 singles and 36 doubles, John Jay creates a focused atmosphere. Located on lower campus on 114th Street and overlooking the main lawns, it’s a great place to live as a first-year and integrate yourself into on-campus life.

**Pros:**

- Since John Jay and Wallach are connected, they share many amenities, including the two dining halls and the esports game lounge. Again, the dining halls are a big deal.
- It’s also quite quiet.
- There are some walk-through doubles. This is great if you want a separate space while still having a roommate. It also gives you the option to put both of your beds in one room and have a work/hangout space in the other.
- The laundry room is accessible, and there are usually machines available.

**Cons:**

- It’s not known for being particularly social.
- The bathrooms are small, single-use, and honestly a bit on the gross side.
- Doubles can be walk-in, which may bother you if your roommate is constantly walking through your room to go to the bathroom at night.
- There are no kitchens and the lounges are a bit small.

## Carman Hall

Carman gets its reputation as the party dorm for a reason; made up of 281 doubles and 18 singles, Carman is extremely social. It’s located on lower campus, on the corner of Broadway and 114th Street.

**Pros:**

- This is notoriously known as the social dorm. People are always hanging out in the hallway, with plenty of parties and pregames happening on any given weekend night. You’re bound to run into people you know in the elevator and lobby.
- The basement laundry room is really great. There’s a nice lounge you can hang out in while you wait for your clothes to finish, and it even has ping-pong tables.
- You get your own bathroom. There are normally four people (and sometimes 10, all in doubles) in each suite, and each suite has a bathroom (10-person suite has two).
- There’s a jazz practice room with a drum kit and a piano, and some empty practice rooms (they don’t have pianos, though).

**Cons:**

- It’s loud, relative to other dorms.
- The lounges are smaller and less comfortable, with no natural light.
- You have to clean your own bathroom, which means things can get … gross.
- The elevators are ridiculously slow, which can make you late.
- A lot of bad views (e.g., facing walls).
- No kitchens, except for one in the basement.

## Furnald Hall

With a convenient spot on lower campus on Broadway between 115th and 116th Streets and overlooking the main lawn, Furnald has 188 singles and 27 doubles, delivering a very cozy and reserved vibe.

**Pros:**

- Furnald is right next to Lerner Hall, which houses Ferris Booth Commons and many other amenities such as the music studio, practice rooms, lounges, study rooms, and Columbia Psychological Services.
- It’s very quiet—again, good for studying and focus.
- The lobby has pool tables, which can be a lot of fun.
- The lounges are nice, with a lot of natural light.
- The basement has bike storage.
- There are kitchens on every floor.

**Cons:**

- Furnald isn’t known for being the most social.
- There are communal bathrooms. There’s one women’s and one men’s bathroom on each floor, each with multiple showers, toilets, and sinks. If privacy is important to you, this could be a problem.
- The laundry room is located in the basement, and residents have complained about broken machines and residents not being respectful of each other's laundry.

## Wien Hall

Located on 116 Street, half a block from the Amsterdam gate, Wien is the furthest housing option for first-years. With 311 singles, 22 doubles, and its slightly farther location, Wien is great for an independent lifestyle.

**Pros:**

- A lot of people say the best thing about living in Wien is the sinks in the dorms. This is extremely helpful for brushing your teeth, washing your face or hands, and cleaning your dishes; it can be a great time-saver in the morning.
- The mail center is inside the building, which makes a big difference during the winter months.
- Faculty House, one of Columbia’s better dining halls, is right next door, and Fac Shack (a dining food truck) is right outside.
- Each floor has a stovetop and a microwave in the lounge (but no oven or storage for kitchen tools).
- There’s a spacious lounge on the ground floor with a grand piano.
- It’s very quiet—good for working.
- The laundry room is located on the second floor and is typically clean, but depending on the day, it can be busy.

**Cons:**

- Wien is currently the only first-year housing option that isn’t in the main quad. It’s probably only a two-minute walk from the Amsterdam gate, but it can be a bummer to not overlook the lawns.
- Bathrooms are fully communal, and some residents complain that they end up dirty.
- Lounges are a bit small but have natural light.
- It’s very non-social and quiet. Everyone mostly keeps to themselves (this could also be a pro, depending on the person).
- While this first-year housing guide seems like a lot, remember, you will find the place for you. No matter which dorm you end up in, you will find your people and develop healthy routines; each dorm is great in its own way. Good luck with registration, and can’t wait to see you on campus!
